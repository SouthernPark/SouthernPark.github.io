** Tell me about a time you dealt with ambiguity
1. 问题: SDN team 一开始的需求很宽泛，想仿真到什么程度
2. Action: identify use cases
3. 发现 Docker VS 轻量快，但是不能解决 全部问题，trade off
4. 反思: 需求清晰的时候，一定要与客户 确定 use cases, 从 use cases 上可以反映出他们的真实需求，以及我们的 系统架构 和 决策
#+begin_src
Situation — The initial ambiguity

"One example that comes to mind is when I was working on our virtual switch project.
Initially, our SDN team came to us with a pretty high-level request. They wanted a way to simulate our data center network.
To be honest, at first, I wasn't entirely sure what they were looking for. There were several ways we could approach this, depending on their actual use cases."

Action — Clarifying the real requirements

"So instead of jumping straight into implementation, I reached out to the SDN team to better understand what they were trying to achieve.
I started by asking whether they mainly wanted to test network protocols, like BGP and LLDP.
If that was the case, I thought a Docker-based virtual switch would be a good fit. It would be relatively lightweight and easy to deploy, and we could run the necessary protocol daemons inside the containers."

Discovery — Understanding the real problem

"But after talking with them, I realized their needs were actually more complicated.
Their main goal was to let network engineers validate configuration changes and software hotfixes in a simulated environment before deploying them to production.
That changed how I thought about the problem."

Technical decision — Evaluating the trade-offs

"For configuration changes, a Docker-based solution could still work. We could extend it with Redis and the necessary CLI tools.
But hotfix testing was a different story. Some hotfixes involved warm restarts, which required interactions with the ASIC layer.
That's when I realized that a lightweight protocol simulation wouldn't be enough. We needed something that could simulate the behavior of the entire switch.
So I proposed using KVM to run the full network OS, together with an ASIC simulator.
It was a heavier solution, but it gave us a more realistic environment for testing both configuration changes and software upgrades."

Reflection:
This experience taught me that dealing with ambiguity isn't about having all the answers upfront. It's about asking the right questions, validating assumptions, and making progress as you learn more.
#+end_src

*** How did you know you had clarified the requirements enough?
与 SDN 团队，确定了 他们的 核心使用案例，核心案例已经帮助我们 确定 整体 架构应该是 使用 KVM 而不是 docker vs。
We had enough information to make the architectural decision
#+begin_src
"I don't think we need to clarify every single detail before starting implementation.
For me, the important thing is to understand the main use cases and the constraints that could affect the architecture.
In this case, once I understood that they needed to test software hotfixes involving warm restarts, I knew that a lightweight Docker-based solution wouldn't be enough.
There were still implementation details to figure out, but we had enough information to make the architectural decision."
#+end_src

*** Why didn't you just build Docker-VS first and extend it later?
强调自己 considered 过了，但是由于 核心需求当中有 hotfix 长期方案 还是 KVM 为主，所以在 在时间 tight 的情况下，主要 focus 还是在 KVM 上

** Tell me about a time you had a disagreement with a coworker. How did you handle it?
Respect: 能否尊重不同观点，而不是立即否定对方。
Open-mindedness: 是否愿意承认自己的判断可能有误。
Data-driven decision-making: 是否通过事实而不是争论解决问题。
Collaboration: 能否让双方一起推动问题解决。
Self-awareness: 是否能够反思自己的沟通方式。

#+begin_src
Situation — What was the disagreement?

"One example that comes to mind is when I was debugging a watermark counter issue on our network switch.
The issue was that after we cleared the watermark counters, the values were not reset as expected.
One of my teammates believed it was a hardware issue, while I suspected it might be related to the software layer."

Conflict — Understanding the other perspective

"He had a reasonable argument. The same feature was working correctly on other platforms, so he believed the software implementation was fine.
I could see where he was coming from, but I didn't think we had enough evidence to rule out the software layer."

Action — How I handled the disagreement

"Instead of going back and forth about whose assumption was correct, I decided to investigate the issue from both sides.
I first checked the hardware counters, but I couldn't find anything that clearly pointed to a hardware problem.
Then I looked into the software path and found that the clear operation wasn't triggering the expected state update in Redis.
I also found a similar issue that had already been fixed in another branch.
I shared my findings with my teammate and walked him through the evidence, rather than simply telling him that his assumption was wrong."

Result — What happened?

"That helped us narrow down the root cause and focus on the software state synchronization issue."

Reflection — What I learned

"Looking back, I learned that technical disagreements are often caused by different assumptions, not necessarily by people being difficult.
If I were to handle it again, I would try to agree on a debugging plan earlier, so we could test both hypotheses systematically.
This experience taught me to focus on evidence and shared goals rather than trying to prove my own point."
#+end_src
