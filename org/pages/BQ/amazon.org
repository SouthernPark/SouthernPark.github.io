** Customer Obsession (客户至上)

Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust. Although leaders pay attention to competitors, they obsess over customers.
对于 Software Engineer 面试，它主要考察的是：你能不能从客户的实际需求出发做技术决策，而不只是完成分配给你的 engineering task。

*** Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer.
#+begin_src
Situation

I was working on a project to deliver a KVM-based virtual switch to our SDN team. The goal was to provide network engineers with a virtual environment where they could practice and validate network configuration changes before applying them to physical switches.
The initial requirements were straightforward: the virtual switch needed to support basic interface configuration and networking protocols such as BGP and LLDP.
During development, I noticed that the KVM took around 15 minutes to boot and become ready for configuration. I brought this up with the SDN team, but they told me that a 15-minute boot time was acceptable because they could create the KVM instances ahead of time.

Task

Even though the customer was willing to accept the boot time, I felt it wasn't a great user experience. Engineers would still have to wait whenever they needed to restart or create a new environment.
So I decided to investigate whether I could improve the boot time without compromising the functionality the SDN team needed.

Action

I started by analyzing the boot logs to identify which operations were taking the most time.
I found that buffer-related operations were a major bottleneck. These configurations were important for physical switches, but they weren't necessary for the KVM's intended use case.
Instead of spending time optimizing those operations, I modified the KVM implementation to bypass the unnecessary buffer operations while preserving the networking functionality the SDN team needed.

Result

As a result, I reduced the KVM boot time from approximately 15 minutes to 6 minutes, a 60% improvement.
The SDN team was very happy with the improvement. More importantly, they started using the KVM for additional testing scenarios beyond the original requirements, including configuration persistence, protocol recovery, and warm restart testing.
As the team found more value in the KVM, I also had the opportunity to take on additional development and maintenance responsibilities for the project.

Reflection

This experience taught me that customer obsession isn't just about meeting explicit requirements. Sometimes customers are willing to accept an inconvenience because they assume it's unavoidable.
As engineers, I should think about how customers actually use our products and proactively look for ways to improve their experience, even when they haven't specifically asked us to.
#+end_src

*** Tell me about a time you had to balance customer needs with technical constraints.
它不仅考察 Customer Obsession，也涉及 Bias for Action、Dive Deep、Deliver Results 和 Earn Trust
在技术限制下，你如何做出合理的工程决策，尽可能满足客户的核心需求。最理想的情况是：客户不需要让步，你通过技术创新或合理的 trade-off 解决了问题。

#+begin_src
Situation

During our switch qualification testing, one of the customer's key requirements was accurate CPU protection rate limiting. They needed to ensure that traffic reaching the CPU stayed within a configured rate to protect the control plane.
However, I ran into a hardware limitation. The ASIC policer used a probabilistic mechanism, which caused the measured traffic rate to fluctuate.
I explained the underlying hardware behavior and its limitations to the customer. However, accurate rate limiting was a strict requirement for their qualification tests, and the switch couldn't pass without meeting it.

Task

My responsibility was to find a solution that could meet the customer's rate-limiting requirements despite the hardware limitation.
Since we couldn't easily change the ASIC's policing behavior, I needed to explore other approaches without compromising control-plane protection.

Action

I started by researching alternative approaches to rate limiting.
I realized that instead of relying entirely on the hardware policer, we could introduce an additional layer of protection on the software side.
The idea was to use the ASIC policer as the first layer to limit incoming traffic, and then apply more precise rate limiting in the kernel using XDP/eBPF.
I studied the existing XDP/eBPF implementation and worked on integrating and adapting it into our network operating system.
This allowed us to combine hardware-level traffic protection with finer-grained software rate limiting, without requiring changes to the ASIC's underlying policing mechanism.

Result

As a result, we were able to meet the customer's CPU protection rate-limiting requirements, and the switch successfully passed the qualification tests.
More importantly, we addressed the hardware limitation without asking the customer to relax their original requirements.

Reflection

This experience taught me that balancing customer needs with technical constraints doesn't necessarily mean asking the customer to compromise.
Sometimes, it means finding a different technical approach that works around the limitation while still delivering what the customer needs.

** Ownership (主人翁精神)
Leaders are owners. They think long term and don’t sacrifice long-term value for short-term results. They act on behalf of the entire company, beyond just their own team. They never say “that’s not my job.”
考察的是你是否能像一个 owner 一样对项目、产品和长期结果负责，而不仅仅是完成分配给你的任务。

*** Tell me about a time you took ownership of something outside your responsibilities.
#+begin_src
Situation:

We encountered a production issue where continuous port flapping could cause certain port counter collection threads to get stuck, eventually leading to a critical process exiting.
The issue was primarily owned by a colleague responsible for port-related features, so it wasn't part of my direct responsibilities.
However, the problem affected the reliability of our production switches, and we suspected it was related to the ASIC firmware. The vendor needed more time to reproduce and investigate the root cause.

Task:

Even though I wasn't the original owner of the issue, I wanted to help reduce the risk to our production switches rather than simply wait for the vendor's investigation.

Action:

I got involved in evaluating possible mitigations while the firmware investigation was ongoing.
We identified that the affected counters were related to FEC statistics. Although these counters were useful for monitoring, they were less critical than keeping the switch's core processes stable.
We decided to introduce a temporary hot patch to disable the affected counter collection, accepting a temporary reduction in monitoring visibility to protect production stability.
Meanwhile, the vendor continued investigating the underlying firmware issue.

Result:

The hot patch provided a temporary mitigation while the vendor worked on a permanent fix.
Later, the vendor identified the root cause and released updated firmware to address the issue.

Reflection:

This experience reinforced my understanding that ownership isn't limited to the components I'm officially responsible for. When a problem affects production reliability, I should be willing to step outside my usual responsibilities and contribute to solving it.

Situation:

I was the owner of the Adaptive Routing (ARS) feature for our new network switch. The goal was to reduce network congestion and improve the performance of AI workloads.
Although the feature was functionally working, during high bandwidth performance testing, we found that our AI workload was only achieving around 62% of the 400G port bandwidth, which was well below our performance target. We also had a tight deadline to deliver the feature.

Task:
As the feature owner, I was responsible for ensuring that the feature worked correctly and met its performance requirements.
I needed to identify the bottleneck and drive the optimization effort across multiple teams to meet our delivery deadline.

Action:
Initially, I focused on tuning the queue depth thresholds to make adaptive routing more responsive to congestion. However, after multiple rounds of testing, we saw little improvement.
Instead of continuing to tune the same parameters, I reached out to the ASIC vendor to investigate other possible factors. Through our technical discussions, the vendor identified another configurable parameter that could influence the adaptive routing behavior.
We incorporated that parameter into our tuning process and continued performance testing.
At the same time, I realized that switch-side optimization alone wasn't enough. The overall performance also depended on the end-host GPU NIC configuration.
So I coordinated with the teams responsible for the GPU NICs to work on end-to-end performance tuning.
Despite the tight deadline and dependencies on multiple teams, I continued driving the investigation and optimization effort toward our performance target.

Result:
Through the combined switch-side and end-host optimizations, we improved the effective bandwidth utilization of our AI workload from 62% to 87% of the 400G port capacity, meeting our performance requirements.
We were able to complete the performance optimization phase and bring the feature to the required level of readiness.

Reflection:

This experience taught me that ownership means being accountable for the final outcome.
Even though my primary responsibility was the switch-side ARS feature, I needed to work across team boundaries and drive the end-to-end optimization effort to deliver the expected performance.

*** Tell me about a time you made a mistake or failed. How did you handle it?

#+begin_src
Situation:

During the bring-up of our new network switch, one of the routing test cases failed.
The test was designed to verify that the switch would prioritize a directly connected neighbor over a BGP route to the same destination.
I checked with the ASIC vendor, and they explained that the observed behavior was their default implementation.
At that point, I assumed the behavior was acceptable and modified the test case to account for the platform difference.
However, when I shared this change with my tech lead during our daily stand-up, he pointed out that this was actually a gating requirement from our network engineering team. We needed consistent basic routing behavior across all switch platforms.
I realized that I had made a mistake by accepting the vendor's default behavior without checking our product requirements.

Task:
I needed to correct my mistake and find a solution that would meet our routing requirements without blocking the release of the new switch.

Action:
I took responsibility for resolving the issue and arranged a technical discussion with the ASIC vendor and my tech lead.
I explained our routing requirements and why the platform-specific behavior was not acceptable.
The vendor confirmed that changing the underlying hardware forwarding behavior was not feasible.
Instead of accepting that limitation, we explored a software-based solution and came up with an approach to dynamically install a higher-priority host route for the directly connected neighbor.
I implemented the solution and worked with the vendor to review the changes and ensure that the routing behavior matched our requirements.

Result:
The solution corrected the routing behavior and resolved the original test failure, allowing us to meet the gating requirement.
More importantly, I took responsibility for the incorrect assumption I had made and followed through until the underlying issue was resolved.

Reflection:

This experience taught me that vendor-default behavior doesn't necessarily mean it's correct for our product.
As an engineer, I should always validate platform-specific behavior against our product requirements before changing test expectations.
It also reinforced that ownership means acknowledging mistakes early and taking responsibility for fixing them rather than simply explaining why they happened.

** Invent and Simplify (创新与简化)
Leaders expect and require innovation and invention from their teams and always find ways to simplify. They are externally aware, look for new ideas from everywhere, and are not limited by “not invented here.” As we do new things, we accept that we may be misunderstood for long periods of time.
能否找到新的、更简单的方法来解决问题，而不是一直沿用现有方案，或者通过增加复杂度来解决问题。
